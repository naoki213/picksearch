A portable blender only earns its spot on the counter if you actually use it — and the fastest way to build that habit is having a few go-to recipes memorized so you're not staring into the fridge every morning. Here are five that fit a standard 300–500ml portable blender jar and come together in about two minutes.

All five follow the same basic ratio: liquid first (so the blades have something to grab), then soft ingredients, then anything frozen or icy last. If you're using a smaller 300ml jar, scale each recipe down by about a third.

## 1. Banana Oat Breakfast Smoothie

- 1 ripe banana
- 2 tbsp rolled oats
- 200ml milk (or oat milk)
- 1 tsp honey

Blend for 30–40 seconds. The oats add just enough texture to keep you full until lunch without needing a spoon.

## 2. Green Apple & Spinach

- 1 handful spinach
- 1/2 green apple, cored
- 150ml water or apple juice
- Squeeze of lemon

This is the one to reach for when you want something that tastes like more effort than it actually took. The apple hides the spinach almost completely.

## 3. Frozen Berry & Yogurt

- 100g frozen mixed berries
- 100ml plain yogurt
- 100ml milk

Blend the berries last so the blades have the easiest ingredients to work through first — it keeps the motor from bogging down on a small portable jar.

## 4. Coffee Banana Protein Shake

- 1 banana
- 150ml cold brew coffee (or strong cooled coffee)
- 1 scoop protein powder
- A few ice cubes

A genuinely useful pre-workout or mid-afternoon option — coffee and banana blend more smoothly than you'd expect, and it doesn't need added sugar.

## 5. Mango Pineapple Cooler

- 100g frozen mango
- 50g pineapple chunks
- 150ml coconut water

The closest thing to a vacation in a bottle. If your blender struggles with two frozen fruits at once, let the mango sit out for a few minutes first.

## A Few Tips for Better Results

- **Liquid first, always.** It's the single biggest fix if your blender is struggling or leaving chunks.
- **Don't overfill.** Stick to the fill line — portable jars are small, and overfilling is the most common reason the lid doesn't seal properly.
- **Frozen fruit over ice** gives a thicker, less watery result, since ice just dilutes the drink as it melts.
